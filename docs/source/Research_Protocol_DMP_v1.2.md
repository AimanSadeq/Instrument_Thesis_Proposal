**RESEARCH PROTOCOL AND DATA MANAGEMENT PLAN**

*Leveraging Artificial Intelligence for Financial Competence Development: A Design-Based Research Study of AI-Assisted Training Design, Gamification, and Organizational Outcomes*

Candidate: Aiman S. Sadeq · Supervisor: Professor Emanuele Borgonovo · Doctorate in Business Administration, SDA Bocconi School of Management

Version 1.2 · August 2026 · Status: draft for supervisor review ahead of the September cohorts

*Supersedes version 1.1. Changes from 1.1 are summarised in Section 10; the changes 1.1 made to 1.0 are retained there as well.*

**1. Purpose and research questions**

The study investigates how generative AI can be leveraged to develop financial competence in organizations, and what design principles govern effective AI-assisted finance training. Five research questions address: (RQ1) the role of AI in producing training artifacts and the effect of AI-human collaboration on their quality; (RQ2) how AI-designed gamification and simulation mechanics influence professionals\' engagement with financial decision-making; (RQ3) the organizational and individual factors that enable or constrain adoption; (RQ4) the extent to which AI-assisted training contributes to perceived financial competence; and (RQ5) the governance principles that should guide responsible use of AI in training design for regulated environments.

**2. Design summary**

Two-cycle Design-Based Research (DBR); qualitative core (Reflexive Thematic Analysis; Framework Method) with bounded descriptive statistics.

Cycle 1 comprises up to four cohorts of the same finance-for-non-finance programme, at three client organizations, and they are not identical in length or in who delivers them.

-   Cohort 1A: approximately 25 adult professionals at a large Saudi technology company, four days, 6 to 9 September 2026, facilitated by the candidate.

-   Cohorts 1B and 1C: two groups at a second Saudi organization, three days each, 6 to 8 and 13 to 15 September 2026, facilitated by a colleague of the candidate rather than by the candidate. Research participation at this organization is subject to the client authorisation described in Section 7 and is not assumed here.

-   Cohort 1D: a third client organization in October 2026, not yet confirmed.

Cohort 1A and Cohort 1B run in the same week and overlap by three days. They are served by separate instances of the instrument platform, each stamping its own cohort label, and every count, export and deletion is scoped to the cohort that performed it, so no cohort can read or delete another's records.

The multi-cohort structure gives Cycle 1 an internal iteration: what the first cohorts reveal about the instruments and the training materials can be acted upon and re-tested inside the cycle rather than deferred to Cycle 2. The variation in programme length and in facilitator is treated as informative rather than as noise, and Section 6 sets out what the second of those two variations does to the dual-role limitation.

Cycle 2 (2026 to 2027) deploys systematically refined materials across multiple organizations with interviews, focus groups, and observation. Full institutional ethics review via the Bocconi ethics portal will be completed before Cycle 2 fieldwork begins.

**3. Participants and recruitment**

Population: adult professionals (non-finance backgrounds) attending a finance-for-non-finance corporate training programme arranged by their employer.

The employer arranges attendance at the TRAINING; participation in the RESEARCH (completing instruments) is individually voluntary. Declining carries no employment consequence, and the consent materials state this explicitly.

No vulnerable groups; no minors.

**4. Instruments and mode of collection**

Four instruments, bilingual in English and Arabic:

-   Consent and Briefing Sheet: research context, supervisor, voluntariness, anonymity, right to withdraw.

-   Pre-Training Questionnaire: demographics, prior finance knowledge, expectations (Day 1, before content).

-   Daily Reflection Cards, one per training day: three open questions per day; the final day adds one cross-programme question.

-   Post-Training Evaluation: Kirkpatrick Levels 1 and 2, design-quality perceptions, gamification experience (Day 4).

Mode of collection. Instruments are delivered as web forms and completed by participants on their own or on provided devices. Access is by a short URL and QR code displayed in the room at the relevant point in the programme. There is no login, no account, and no invitation by email or name, so no participant list or contact record is created at any stage.

The instrument layer is deliberately separate from the FinPlay training platform. Participants log in to FinPlay to take part in the training; they do not log in to complete research instruments. The two systems are not connected, and no FinPlay identity, session, or telemetry is associated with any instrument response.

Programme length. The instruments are written for a four-day programme and that text is canonical. Where a cohort runs a different number of days, the instruments are served with the day count substituted and nothing else altered: the programme length in the briefing, the day the final evaluation falls on, the range in the daily reflection title, the number of options in the day selector, and the day that carries the cross-programme question. Part 5 of Research Instruments v2.1 specifies the substitution in both languages. No item wording, response option or ordering varies with length. Each stored reflection records how many days its programme ran, and the database will not accept the cross-programme question on any day but the last of them.

Paper versions of every instrument are printed and carried to each session as an operational fallback (Section 8), matched to that cohort's programme length. If the fallback is used, completed sheets are placed unfolded and unmarked into a collection box which is sealed in the room before it leaves, as described in the facilitator run sheet, and the substitution is recorded in the deviations log. This supersedes the sealed-envelope-per-participant procedure of version 1.0: an envelope for each person reintroduces exactly the per-person object the design exists to avoid.

All instruments are anonymous by design: no names, employee numbers, or other identifiers are collected anywhere.

**5. Consent procedure**

On Day 1, before any teaching content, the facilitator reads a standardized briefing identifying the doctoral research, the university and supervisor, the voluntary character of participation, and the anonymity guarantee. Participants receive a printed information page to keep. This page is given to participants and nothing is collected from it.

Participants then open the consent screen. It presents two options with equal prominence: to take part, or not to take part. Both options are submitted in the same way, and both return the same closing screen, so that every person in the room performs the same visible sequence of actions on their device.

Those who decline are not prompted again: the confirmation screen is terminal and identical for both choices, and no instrument links to another. Each instrument is opened only from the link displayed in the room at the relevant moment, which a declining participant simply does not open. This is stated in these terms deliberately. Suppressing later instruments on a declining participant's own device would require recognising that device on a subsequent visit, by cookie, stored value, URL token or fingerprint, every one of which Section 6 forbids. The protocol therefore claims what the design delivers: the software prompts nobody.

Consent covers all instruments across all training days. Each instrument completed after the Day 1 briefing opens with a one-line reminder that participation remains voluntary; version 2.0 of the instruments omitted this from the pre-training questionnaire and version 2.1 corrects it. Withdrawal is exercised simply by not submitting a subsequent instrument.

**One honest difference from version 1.0.** Under the paper procedure a declining participant returned a blank form that was physically indistinguishable from a completed one, so no record of declining existed at all. Under the online procedure a decline is recorded as an anonymous count. It cannot be attributed to any individual, because no identifiers, IP addresses, or session data are held. The practical effect is that declining remains invisible to the facilitator and to the employer, while the study gains an accurate participation rate. This is judged an acceptable and arguably favourable exchange, but it is a change and is recorded as one.

**6. Anonymity and the facilitator-researcher dual role**

The candidate both delivers the programme and conducts the research in Cohort 1A, a standard configuration in Design-Based Research that this protocol manages explicitly rather than assumes away.

In Cohorts 1B and 1C the configuration does not arise. A colleague delivers those programmes; the candidate neither facilitates nor observes them and is not present in the room. The controls below still apply, because they are properties of the instruments rather than of who is standing at the front, but the dual role itself is absent rather than mitigated. Those cohorts therefore provide a comparison the study did not originally have: the same materials and the same instruments, with and without the designer in the room.

Anonymity is established at source by the following technical and procedural controls:

-   No login, no user account, no session token, and no cookie is used by the instrument layer.

-   Each submission is written independently. No linkage exists between instruments, between days, or between any instrument and the FinPlay platform.

-   No IP address and no user-agent string is retained.

-   Submission times are recorded at day-level granularity only. Finer timestamps are not stored, because in a cohort of about 25 completing at the same moment a precise submission time could become deductively identifying when combined with demographic bands.

-   Demographic items are categorical and coarse (role band, experience band) for the same reason.

-   Instruments are self-administered. The facilitator does not assist with completion, does not observe individual screens, and does not circulate during completion.

The consequence is that the facilitator cannot know who wrote what, and no response can be linked to an individual. The data are anonymous at source, not anonymized afterwards.

The same design necessarily forecloses two things within Cycle 1: responses cannot be joined across the four days for any individual, and no longitudinal follow-up of individuals is possible. Both were accepted deliberately in favour of the anonymity guarantee.

The candidate maintains a reflexivity journal and a deviations log as part of the audit trail, reviewed in supervision.

**7. Data management plan**

Collection. Responses are submitted through the web forms described in Section 4 and written to a hosted database. Submission counts are logged per instrument per day.

**Hosting and jurisdiction.** The application and database are hosted in the United States (Render and Supabase). This is stated plainly rather than smoothed over. No personal data enters the dataset, so the research data itself involves no cross-border transfer of personal data within the meaning of the KSA Personal Data Protection Law.

Connection metadata, principally IP addresses, is processed transiently at the network layer by the hosting infrastructure, as it is for any online instrument including the widely used hosted survey platforms. It is not logged by the application, not stored in the database, and not associated with any response. Participants never connect to the database at all: only the application server holds a database connection, so no participant address can appear in a database log. Any edge logging performed by the hosting provider is outside the study's control, is not accessible to the researcher, and forms no part of the dataset. Version 1.1 asserted that such metadata was "not logged, not retained" without qualification, which was a claim about a third party that the study cannot evidence; this wording claims only what the design supports.

Client authorisation. Before each cohort, the client organization\'s coordinator confirms that completion of externally hosted research forms on the client network or on personal devices is permitted under the organization\'s own data policy. Confirmation is recorded in the audit trail.

Export and storage. Responses are exported within 48 hours of the final training day (target 11 September 2026 for Cohort 1). Exports are stored encrypted with access restricted to the candidate. Once an export is verified complete, the source records are deleted from the hosted database, so that no research data remains on third-party infrastructure beyond the period required to collect it.

Analysis records (codes, matrices, memos) reference response IDs only. Each response carries a random identifier generated when the record is written, deliberately random rather than sequential so that it reveals nothing about the order in which people submitted, and carrying no meaning outside the dataset. Version 1.1 described these as generated at export; the property being protected is unchanged, and this states the mechanism as built.

KSA Personal Data Protection Law alignment: data minimization (anonymous by design), purpose limitation (research and programme improvement only, stated in the briefing), security of processing, and the position on cross-border transfer set out above.

No participant responses are entered into generative AI systems. AI tools are used in the research only as documented in the study\'s AI-use disclosure, and never with raw participant data.

Retention: for the duration of the doctoral research; anonymized digital data retained per SDA Bocconi requirements, then deleted. Any paper originals arising from use of the fallback are locked and destroyed after thesis acceptance.

**8. Risks and mitigations**

-   Perceived workplace pressure to participate. Explicit voluntariness script; equal prominence of both consent options; no employer access to individual responses, and none exist.

-   Facilitator influence on responses. Self-administration; no facilitator observation of screens; no identifiers; no linkage.

-   Perception that responses are tracked because collection is digital. The briefing states explicitly that the instruments are separate from FinPlay, require no login, and record no IP address or identity. The absence of a login screen is itself visible evidence of this to participants.

-   Deductive identification in a small cohort. Coarse demographic categories; day-level timestamps only; reporting at theme level, never at individual-response level with identifying detail.

-   Network failure, device shortage, or client network restrictions. Printed copies of every instrument, matched to that cohort's programme length, are carried to each session. The facilitator reverts to the paper procedure described in Section 4 for the affected instrument, and records the substitution in the deviations log.

-   The wrong pack, or the wrong service, in a week when two cohorts overlap. Each printed pack states its own programme length on the daily reflection sheet, and each instance of the platform states its cohort and its programme length on the facilitator's page. Both are checked before anything is displayed to a room.

-   Unequal device access among participants. Devices are made available so that participation does not depend on owning a suitable personal device.

-   Bilingual rendering. Arabic right-to-left presentation is tested across the device types and browsers expected in the room before the session, and the paper fallback covers any failure.

-   Data loss. Export within 48 hours; encrypted redundant storage; verification of export completeness before any source deletion.

**10. Summary of changes**

*From version 1.1 to version 1.2*

-   Section 2: Cycle 1 grows from two cohorts at two organizations to up to four at three, with two programme lengths, and two of them delivered by a colleague rather than by the candidate. Two cohorts overlap in the same week and are served by separate instances with cohort-scoped counts, exports and deletion.

-   Section 4: the daily reflection is one card per training day rather than four, and the cross-programme question attaches to the final day rather than to Day 4. The substitution that produces a shorter programme is specified, and pointed at Part 5 of Research Instruments v2.1.

-   Section 4: the paper fallback now follows the collection-box procedure of the facilitator run sheet rather than the sealed-envelope-per-participant procedure of version 1.0, which reintroduced a per-person object.

-   Section 5: the sentence about declining participants no longer implies a technical control that cannot exist without breaching Section 6, and says instead what the design delivers, that the software prompts nobody.

-   Section 5: the voluntariness reminder is recorded as appearing on every instrument after the Day 1 briefing, which Research Instruments v2.1 corrects and v2.0 did not satisfy.

-   Section 6: the facilitator-researcher dual role is scoped to the cohort the candidate delivers, and its absence in the colleague-delivered cohorts is identified as a comparison rather than passed over.

-   Section 7: the claim that connection metadata is "not logged, not retained" is narrowed to what the study can evidence, and strengthened where it can be: participants never connect to the database at all.

-   Section 7: response identifiers are described as generated when the record is written, which is how they are built, rather than at export.

-   Sections 8 and 9: the risks of two overlapping cohorts, and a timeline covering all of them.

*From version 1.0 to version 1.1, retained for the audit trail*

-   Section 2: Cycle 1 became two cohorts (September and October 2026) rather than a single pilot cohort.

-   Section 4: instruments moved from paper to online web forms, with no login and no connection to the FinPlay platform. A printed fallback set was retained for operational failure.

-   Section 5: the sealed-envelope return channel was replaced by online submission with two equally prominent consent options. The change to how declining is recorded was stated explicitly rather than left implicit.

-   Section 6: anonymity controls were restated in technical terms appropriate to online collection, and the resulting loss of within-cohort linkage and longitudinal follow-up was acknowledged.

-   Section 7: added hosting jurisdiction, the treatment of connection metadata, client authorisation, export and source deletion. The cross-border transfer statement was narrowed to what the design supports.

-   Section 8: added risks arising from digital collection, namely perceived tracking, network and device failure, unequal device access, and bilingual rendering.

-   Section 9: timeline extended to cover the October cohort and the build and testing of the instrument layer.
