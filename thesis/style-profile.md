# Aiman's Writing Voice — extracted from the Year 1 Literature Review (D10)

Binding for ALL thesis-proposal prose from v13 onward. Derived from close reading of
DBA8 Literature Review (Sept 2025), the document Professor Borgonovo has already read.

## The fingerprint

1. **Direct, plain openings.** Subject first, verb early. "One important theory is the
   Efficient Market Hypothesis." "These tools were important, but they often had limited
   use." Never open with an abstract frame ("The phenomena under investigation...").
2. **Linear sentences, moderate length.** One idea per sentence, at most one dependent
   clause. No stacked appositives, no double parentheticals, no interrupted syntax.
3. **First person, active.** "I will present three examples." "We discuss in this section
   four major theories." The researcher is visibly present and owns the choices.
4. **Explicit signposting and enumeration.** "This section shows how..." "The following are
   five factors..." "Three broad conclusions emerge. First... Second... Third..." Uses
   numbered or lettered lists where the content is list-like instead of burying lists in prose.
5. **Teacherly inline definitions.** Technical terms are explained immediately, in plain
   words, often in parentheses, even when a specialist would not need it.
6. **Plain connectives.** However, That said, In addition, Nonetheless, Similarly,
   At the same time, Accordingly. Never "thereby", "wherein", "heretofore".
7. **Citations do the work as sentence subjects.** "(Kaiser et al., 2022) find that..."
   with simple reporting verbs: show, find, demonstrate, examine, stress, confirm.
8. **Occasional rhetorical question** to frame a problem ("Can decision-makers trust
   models they do not fully understand?").
9. **Concrete verbs over nominalizations.** "Financial education works" rather than
   "the intervention demonstrates efficacy". Says the plain thing.
10. **Emphasis by restatement,** not literary flourish.

## Banned (from my earlier draft's register)

- Em dashes anywhere (D9: replace with period, comma, or plain dash by sentence logic).
- Literary metaphors: "the empirical base thins abruptly", "around the whole runs...",
  "the hinge of the DBR logic", "descends from".
- Showy verbs: codified -> defined/set out; architected -> defined; underscores -> shows;
  anchored by -> based on/built on (use sparingly); interrogates -> examines.
- Abstract stand-ins for people/things: "the phenomena under investigation",
  "the study's population", "the outcome construct" (say "financial competence").
- Word-order inversions and fronted subordinate clauses used for elegance.

## Rewrite constraints (unchanged)

- All citations, facts, numbers, cross-references, and decisions D1-D8 preserved exactly.
- American spelling (D6). Section/heading structure unchanged.
- Word count must not shrink: the band floor is 8,000 words excluding references and
  appendices. The plain style tends to run slightly longer (definitions, signposts) - good.
