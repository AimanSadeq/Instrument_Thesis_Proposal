# Project description for ECR submission

**Project title:** AI-assisted design of corporate finance training: quality, engagement
and outcomes of a gamified program for non-finance professionals

**Researcher:** Aiman Sadeq, DBA candidate, SDA Bocconi School of Management
**Supervisor:** Prof. Emanuele Borgonovo, Bocconi University
**Submission track:** expedite (self-assessment checklist: no element flagged), requested
as an umbrella project covering both research cycles described below.

## Purpose

The study examines how generative artificial intelligence can be used, under expert human
review, to design corporate finance training, and how well the resulting program performs
in live professional delivery. The training itself, a finance program for non-finance
professionals built around a team-based business simulation, is delivered commercially as
part of participants' professional development and runs regardless of the research. The
research evaluates the AI-designed materials' quality against established instructional
design principles, participants' engagement with the gamified format, learning outcomes
at Kirkpatrick Levels 1 and 2, and what expert facilitators change when AI-designed
materials meet a real room.

## Design

Design-Based Research in two cycles. Cycle 1 (September to October 2026): the program is
delivered to cohorts of approximately 25 adult professionals at client organizations in
Saudi Arabia; anonymous instruments collect participant feedback, and two practitioners
involved in delivery are interviewed. Findings drive a documented redesign of the
materials. Cycle 2 (late 2026 to 2027): refined materials are deployed across multiple
organizations with interviews, focus groups and observation, under the same ethical
arrangements, with any design change that could flag a checklist element re-submitted to
the ECR as the umbrella terms require.

## Participants and procedures

Two groups, under different and explicitly separated regimes.

**Learners** are adult professionals attending the training at external venues, nominated
by their employers, whose written permission for the research is obtained in advance and
recorded. Participation is individually consented and takes up to four short activities
on the participant's own device: a questionnaire at the start of Day 1 (about 5 minutes),
a two-minute reflection at the close of each training day, and an evaluation on the final
day (about 10 minutes). Declining is procedurally invisible: forms are completed
privately, taking part and not taking part look identical, and neither the facilitator
nor the employer can know who participated.

**Practitioners:** two professionals involved in delivering or commissioning the program
are invited to one semi-structured interview each (about 45 minutes) on how the
AI-designed materials performed in delivery, with separate written consent, on the
explicit basis that declining changes nothing commercially or professionally.

## Data and privacy

Learner responses are anonymous at source: no name, identifier, contact detail, IP
address or device identifier is collected; there is no login; and no submission can be
linked to a person, to another submission, or to anything done in the training software,
which runs on a deliberately separate platform. Anonymous data of this kind is not
personal data within the meaning of the GDPR (Recital 26). Interview materials are
confidential rather than anonymous: transcribed personally by the researcher without
automated transcription services, pseudonymized at transcription, organizations unnamed,
quotations checked with the speaker before use. Storage is encrypted and
access-restricted; retention and destruction follow the collection protocol (Research
Protocol and Data Management Plan v1.4, uploaded with this submission). Publication is
in aggregate for learners and under pseudonyms for practitioners; no identifying
information of any participant or client organization is disclosed.

## Ethics posture

The researcher has completed the university's research-ethics course (certificate
uploaded), prepared the informed consent through the ECR portal (uploaded), and completed
the self-assessment checklist with no element flagged, making this submission optional
under the committee's rule and eligible for the expedite track. It is made nonetheless to
place the project under committee oversight as an umbrella covering both cycles.
Foreseeable risks do not exceed those of ordinary daily life; there is no deception, no
compensation, no vulnerable population, and no externally financed component of the
research.
