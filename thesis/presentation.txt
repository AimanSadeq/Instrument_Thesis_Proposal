===== SLIDE 2 =====
PROBLEM STATEMENT
THE PROBLEM
Why current financial literacy training falls short - and why AI-driven gamification needs design principles
PRACTICAL
Training that doesn't stick
Financial literacy programs still rely on lectures, slide decks, and pen-and-paper workshops. Engagement is low, retention is weak, and learners rarely change real financial behaviour.
THEORETICAL
An under-studied intersection
Gamification and AI are well-researched separately, but there is no validated framework for how AI-driven gamification should be designed for financial literacy - what mechanics work, for whom, and when.
OPPORTUNITY
AI changes what's possible
Generative AI now enables personalised scenarios, adaptive difficulty, and real-time feedback at scale - but educators lack design principles to harness it effectively for financial learning.
Conventional training fails to change behaviour, yet there is no validated design framework for AI-driven gamification in financial literacy.

THE
 
RESEARCH
 
GAP
What don't we know? 
Gap 1
AI in Training Design
No published study has examined generative AI as the primary designer of finance training artefacts for professional learners.
Zero studies identified in systematic Scopus review.
Gap 2
Gamification in Finance
Gamification is rarely applied to finance education for professionals. Sailer and Homner (2020) confirm its effectiveness in general learning; application to finance competence remains unexplored.
No studies combine gamification + finance professional training.
Gap 3
The Convergence Gap
No study integrates AI-assisted design + gamification + finance professional training + DBR methodology in a single research design.
This thesis fills that confirmed convergence gap.

GOVERNANCE
 
PROTOCOL
Responsible-AI and Data-Protection Design Assurance (RQ5): using platforms requires abiding by local rules and regulations
AI Design-Assurance Protocol
Human-in-the-Loop Review
All AI-generated artefacts reviewed and fact-checked by subject-matter expert before deployment. Errors and biases logged in AI prompt/output audit trail.
Source Transparency
AI model, prompts, and outputs disclosed in methodology appendix. Versioned change logs maintained across both DBR cycles.
Alignment Sources
UNESCO (2023) Guidance on Generative AI in Education and Research; OECD AI Principles; U.S. Dept. of Education AI Report (2023)
Data Protection Compliance
KSA PDPL (Implementing Regulations 2023)
Consent and notice; data minimisation; purpose limitation; secure storage; cross-border transfer controls documented in ethics appendix.
UAE PDPL (Federal Law No. 45 of 2021)
Applicable where participants are UAE-based or data transits UAE jurisdiction. Same core principles: consent, minimisation, access rights.
Practical Controls
Anonymised participant data; consent forms issued before each cohort; recordings/LMS logs stored with restricted access; retained only for research duration.

===== SLIDE 23 =====
RESEARCH INSTRUMENTS
Data Collection Tools and Example Questions
Trainer / SME Interviews
“Walk me through where you kept and overruled AI suggestions in the PowerPoints and instructor manual. What made a suggestion trustworthy?”
“How did AI help or hinder building case studies, assessments, and exercises - and what did you change after pilot delivery?”
“What would make you keep using these AI-designed materials?” (TAM/UTAUT: usefulness, ease, social influence, facilitating conditions.)
Learner Focus Groups
“Compare your experience with AI-designed eLearning vs previous modules.” (Probe: clarity, feedback, cognitive load.)
“Did gamified elements (leaderboards, badges, timed challenges) help you persist or learn better? Why?” (Probe: autonomy, competence, relatedness.)
Workshop Observation Guide
Note where AI-designed slides or case prompts catalyse peer discussion; where facilitators deviate from AI draft plans; and how learners respond during exercises.
Maps to Kirkpatrick L1–L3 evidence.
Artefact Audit Rubrics
Slides / eLearning
Coherence, signaling, segmenting, modality, personalisation (Mayer)
Instructor Manual
Objective clarity, activity alignment, anticipated misconceptions (Gagné)
Gamification
MDA mapping (mechanic→dynamic→aesthetic) + SDT support (autonomy, competence, relatedness)
