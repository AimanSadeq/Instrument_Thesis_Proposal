## Reasoning on the two judgment items

**1b.** Participants are adult professionals, individually consenting, whose
participation or non-participation is invisible to their employer by design: the forms
are completed privately on participants' own devices, taking part and declining look
identical, and no record of who participated exists. The employer's written permission
covers the conduct of the research alongside its training, not control of access to
persons: participants are free adults reachable outside the employment relationship, and
the sessions take place at an external venue (a hotel), not on the employer's premises.
The item's own examples, incarcerated and hospitalized individuals, indicate custodial
settings in which a gatekeeper controls members' lives; that is not this setting.
Employer authorizations are nevertheless obtained in writing and recorded
(CLIENT_AUTHORISATIONS.md) as good practice, not as gatekeeper control.

**6c.** The learner instruments are anonymous at source: no name, identifier, contact
detail, IP address or device identifier is collected, and no submission can be linked to
a person or to another submission. Anonymous data is not personal data within the
meaning of the GDPR (Recital 26), so the external infrastructure hosting the collection
platform processes no personal data. The practitioner interview materials never touch an
external provider: recordings are transcribed personally by the researcher, without
automated transcription services, and stored encrypted under the researcher's sole
control.

## Standing conditions

This assessment holds for Cycle 1 as designed. It is reassessed, and submission made if
required, if any of the following changes: the instruments stop being anonymous at
source; personal data of any kind is collected through external providers; a client
organization's role changes from permitting the research to directing it; or Cycle 2's
multi-organization design introduces any flagged element. External financing of the
research (item 8) remains none; the commercial training engagements finance the training,
not the research.
