Dear Emanuele,

Draft 1 is attached, with the protocol and the instruments that go with it.

Before anything else, the design has changed since I wrote to you on 21 August, and
one of the changes is significant enough that I would rather put it at the top than
have you find it in Section 4. What you approved was a single September cohort,
delivered by me, in which every participant was anonymous at source.

WHAT MOVED

A second client confirmed two further cohorts, 6 to 8 and 13 to 15 September. They run
three days rather than four, and a colleague delivers both. I am neither present nor
observing. Their participation in the research still depends on that client's written
authorisation, which I have requested and not yet received, so Sections 3 and 4 are
written so that nothing depends on it. Cycle 1 stands on the first cohort alone.

Two of those cohorts being delivered by someone who did not design the materials gives
Cycle 1 something the original design could not offer: the same programme and the same
instruments, with and without the designer in the room. I have written that up
conservatively rather than enthusiastically. Section 4.6 claims it, then immediately
names the four things that vary at once, facilitator, organisation, sector and
programme length, and says I do not treat facilitator identity as isolated.

THE CHANGE THAT NEEDS YOUR VIEW

I have added two interviews to Cycle 1, one with the colleague who delivers and one
with a client training contact. They exist because the learner instruments cannot
reach two of the three things the redesign is supposed to refine: how design work was
divided between the AI and me, and what governs whether an organisation adopts
materials of this kind. Learners see neither, so without these the reflection phase
that drives the whole Design-Based Research logic would rest on one leg of three.

The cost is that the study is no longer anonymous throughout. An interview cannot be
anonymous to the interviewer. Those two participants are known to me and are offered
confidentiality instead, which is a weaker promise, and the protocol says so in those
terms rather than blurring it. Section 6A of the protocol is new and covers the whole
strand; Section 10 lists every change from the version you last saw.

This is what I have taken to Ulyana and Professor Di Stefano, with you copied. It seemed to me that
adding identifiable participants to the first cycle is not a call I should make alone,
and I have said so to them.

ONE THING I REMOVED RATHER THAN ADDED

Section 4.5 previously promised descriptive statistics on pre and post assessment
scores in Cycle 1. It cannot deliver them. The instruments carry no linkage between
submissions, deliberately, so no individual's before can be joined to their after.
Paired gain scores are not merely unbuilt, they are foreclosed by the anonymity
guarantee. I have cut the claim and said why, and accepted that RQ4 rests on perceived
competence in Cycle 1. I would rather lose the measure than weaken the guarantee that
makes declining the research invisible.

WHERE IT IS NOT FINISHED

Thirteen of the seventy-five references need author lists and titles completed from
database exports. They are marked in the text so you can see them. Everything else is
complete: the body is 9,236 words against the 8,000 to 11,000 band, the cover carries
the exact count including references, and the structure follows section 3.2.

WHAT WOULD HELP MOST

If your reading time is limited, Sections 4.6 and 4.7 are where I would spend it. The
grading criteria weight the discussion of advantages and limitations, and those two
sections are where I have tried hardest and where I am least able to judge whether I
have over-claimed or over-hedged. Section 2.8, the convergence gap, is the other place
where an outside eye would help, since the claim that no study combines these four
elements is the one a reader is most likely to test.

I will work on the references and the polish while your comments are with me, and Draft
2 follows in the week of 19 September, which leaves clear room before the 30th.

With kind regards,

Aiman Sadeq
Doctoral candidate, SDA Bocconi School of Management
